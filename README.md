A clinical trial is a type of medical research study that uses human volunteers to test new medical, surgical, or behavioral treatments. [1]  
Purpose 

• Check safety: Learn if a new drug, device, or procedure is safe for people to use. 
• Test effectiveness: See if the new option actually works better than older methods or a placebo. 
• Find early answers: Discover better ways to prevent, diagnose, or screen for diseases. [1, 2, 3, 4]  

The Four Phases 

• Phase I: Tests a new treatment on a small group of people (often healthy volunteers) to find a safe dose and spot side effects. 
• Phase II: Expands the group to hundreds of patients to see how well the treatment works and monitor side effects. 
• Phase III: Tests thousands of patients in different regions to confirm effectiveness and compare it to standard treatments. 
• Phase IV: Monitors safety and long-term effects after the treatment is approved and sold to the general public. [3, 5]
